# 09 — Market and competitor analysis: what Gluons must actually outperform

## Current adoption signals — do not confuse adoption with paid automation spend
- UK government's 2026 AI adoption survey: 32% of UK businesses used at least one AI technology versus 16% in 2025; for many, adoption remains shallow. 65% of non-adopters cited a lack of clear business case or proven ROI, and 81% of surveyed businesses reported some human input or oversight of AI-generated outputs. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-survey-2026/ai-adoption-survey-report-2026-executive-summary
- U.S. Chamber of Commerce 2026 small-business report states 66% use AI and 41% use at least six technology platforms, but the survey is a different population/method. Do **not** average these two adoption percentages. Source: https://www.uschamber.com/technology/2026-empowering-small-business-report
- The UK survey reports nearly half of respondents who could estimate 2025 spending reported no AI expenditure, suggesting AI use and willingness to pay for standalone AI SaaS are not the same.

## Competitive matrix: stronger than a generic 'AI plugin'
| Competitor | Primary verified existing capabilities | Why Gluons cannot claim novelty solely on this | Potential defendable wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zapier MCP | 9,000+ apps, tens of thousands of actions/triggers; agent discovers tools, can save flows as skills, supports host switching and execution history; next-generation Zaps can be deployed from supported AI clients in early access | This already closely resembles 'one subscription, one agent, many automations' | Certified domain operations, native task-level semantic state/approvals, evidence-grade shadow trials, and hands-on underserved SMB activation |
| n8n | Visual workflows, AI-assisted creation and external orchestration, customizable/self-hosted ecosystem | Natural-language workflow generation itself not enough | Nontechnical owner experience and independently validated industry outcomes |

## Counterpositioning
Bad: 'First MCP plugin for every automation.' Competitors already provide agent-controlled actions, skills and recurring workflows.
Better: '**The verified operations layer for AI assistants.** Start from expert-tested business procedures, prove them in shadow mode on your own data, then let the assistant operate them under one set of permissions, spending limits and audit records.'

## Differentiation must be measurable
1. **Skill certificates:** list exact last-tested connector versions, jurisdiction, accuracy, unsupported edge cases, domain reviewer and run receipts. Certificates are test evidence, not marketing badges.
2. **Owner-specific proof:** replay and shadow trials with measured corrections and error rates, not a fabricated '100 hours saved' dashboard.
3. **Trust by design:** expert-specified exception routing, safe approval gates, ability to reconcile in-flight and terminated work.
4. **Incremental automation economics:** first connection produces several independent operational workflows; actually track setup labor per additional automation.
5. **Distribution to skeptics:** onsite onboarding using consent-first demos; evaluate trial conversion, retention and acquisition costs against self-serve channel.
6. **Cross-model independence:** include native fallback so incompatible ChatGPT tier/host does not stop the business operation, and make provider data boundaries transparent.

## Likely reasons the business could fail
- Connecting to proprietary industry systems requires partner approval, bespoke contracts or prohibited scraping workarounds.
- Domain experts and ongoing skill testing cost more than customers will pay.
- Cheaper hosted LLM tokens do not reduce the costs of voice, physical logistics or professional review.
- Customers prefer an existing automation platform bundled into their current SaaS rather than new Gluons fees.
- More tools and more freedom create a security, permissions and quality problem for novice buyers.
- Field sales leads to excessive CAC relative to low subscriptions.
- Chat host access is inconsistent across plans; AI subscriptions do not cover server-side model usage.

## Experiments to validate the defensible thesis
1. A/B: general tool-based agent vs Gluons vetted skill on the same real task; measure verified task success, incorrect external actions, total labor and cost.
2. A/B: read-only sandbox, shadow trial, live demonstration as customer entry; measure confidence, conversion, time-to-first-value and retained use.
3. Within-customer experiment: first automation integration labor vs second/third automation integration labor with same connected systems. Prove reuse.
4. Control UX: owner asks two different assistants to inspect and pause one task; compare success with existing solutions.
5. Field rep vs self-service acquisition: cost per retained active customer after support and implementation costs.

Without wins on these experiments, Gluons is likely a broader interface atop generic automation platforms, not yet a defensible category leader.
